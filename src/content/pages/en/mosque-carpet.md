## Types of mosque carpet by location

Mosque carpet needs differ from one building to the next. A small neighbourhood mosque and a large congregational mosque need different thicknesses and layouts. We choose thickness and area after a site visit.

### Neighbourhood mosques

A neighbourhood mosque hosts the five daily prayers on a steady rhythm. It suits carpet that handles frequent foot traffic and cleans easily. We measure the whole prayer hall. We cut the carpet to that size so the floor is covered without gaps.

### Grand mosques

A grand mosque has a wide hall and large crowds on Fridays and at Eid. It needs a thickness that handles crowding. The 4 m (13.1 ft) roll means fewer joins across a wide hall. We plan the roll layout before cutting so lines run straight from end to end. The larger the area, the more exact the site measuring must be.

### Women's prayer area

The women's prayer area is often smaller and sees less traffic than the main hall. We consider comfort for sitting and prostration, and easy cleaning. Mosque management can choose a different colour from the main hall.

### Company or school musalla

A musalla is a small prayer room. We supply prayer room carpet for companies and schools, including portable musallas. This has its own page on the site.

## How we set the qibla direction and rows

The qibla is the direction Muslims face in prayer. Carpet lines run parallel to the qibla so worshippers stand in straight rows. We find the qibla on site using the mihrab, the niche that marks the direction, and the mosque's own markings. Row width is 1.33 m (4.4 ft), the width of the narrow roll. We measure the hall length and width in metres on site. Then we check the columns, openings and wall corners. If a wall is not square to the qibla, we cut the edges at an angle that keeps the lines straight. We confirm the measurements with mosque management before cutting, because a cut cannot be undone.

## Installation stages

Installation follows this order.

1. Check the floor for levelness, cleanliness and cracks.
2. Check moisture near the wudu area, where worshippers wash before prayer. Moisture affects how well the carpet stays fixed.
3. Prepare the floor by cleaning it and removing dust.
4. Roll out the carpet and align the lines with the qibla.
5. Cut around walls, columns and entrances.
6. Fix the carpet with Butex, double-sided tape or metal carpet end strips, depending on the floor and location.
7. Check the lines and edges before handover.

For two days after installation, we follow up to fix any installation error.

## Cost of carpeting a mosque

Cost = (area in m² × price per m² for the thickness) + installation.

The area is calculated after measuring on site. Mosque carpet prices are the same for the 1.33 m (4.4 ft) roll and the 4 m (13.1 ft) roll:

- Light thickness: 6 KWD per m² (0.557 KWD per sq ft)
- Medium thickness: 7 KWD per m² (0.650 KWD per sq ft)
- Thick: 8 KWD per m² (0.743 KWD per sq ft)

These prices are for mosque carpet only. Turkish carpet and wall-to-wall carpet for other uses cost 5 to 15 KWD per m² (0.465 to 1.394 KWD per sq ft), by thickness, pile pressure and density.

**Example: what does it cost to carpet a 180 m² mosque?** A prayer hall 15 m (49.2 ft) long and 12 m (39.4 ft) wide is 180 m² (1,937.5 sq ft). The carpet costs 1,080 KWD in light, 1,260 KWD in medium or 1,440 KWD in thick, before installation. This is an illustration. The real area is set by measuring on site, net of columns and the mihrab.

Installation is negotiated based on the area and location.

## How to choose the right mosque carpet

**Thickness and number of worshippers.** Choosing mosque carpet starts with how many people pray and how often they come and go. More worshippers call for a thicker carpet that handles heavy foot traffic. A small, quiet neighbourhood mosque may be fine with the light thickness. A mosque that fills on Fridays needs the medium or thick option. Some customers search for mosque rugs. It is the same carpet. We suggest a thickness once we know the area and the approximate number of worshippers. Busy seasons such as Ramadan also matter, so we factor them in.

**Colour and dirt.** The carpet comes in more than 9 colours. Light colours show dust and shoe marks quickly. Dark colours hide some dirt but show lint and hair. Choose a colour that suits the mosque's lighting and style. We discuss colour with you during the site visit. Colour does not replace regular cleaning.

**Maintenance.** Regular vacuuming keeps carpet in mosque halls looking good. Treat stains as soon as they happen. Keep the floor dry near the wudu area. A shoe area outside the prayer hall reduces dust. After installation, we follow up for two days to fix any installation error.
